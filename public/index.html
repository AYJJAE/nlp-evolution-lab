The Distributional Hypothesis

J.R. Firth (1957)
"You shall know a word by the company it keeps."

Word embeddings (like Word2Vec and GloVe) represent words as continuous vectors based on the contexts in which they appear.

The Antonym Paradox:

Why do opposites like hot and cold or good and bad have high cosine similarity? Because they appear in identical grammatical patterns:

  • "The coffee was too hot to drink."
  • "The coffee was too cold to drink."

Because their surrounding contexts are interchangeable, the model places them near each other in vector space!

Static Embeddings Limitation (Polysemy):

In static embeddings, a word has exactly one vector. The word "bank" in "river bank" and "investment bank" receives the identical vector! This polysemy problem was the fundamental catalyst for the invention of Transformers.

Self-Attention Mechanics

Vaswani et al. (2017)
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$
⚠️ Critical Educational Caveat

Attention Is Not (Always) Explanation: High attention weight between two tokens indicates where intermediate representation matrices pooled values, but attention does not guarantee causality or feature necessity in the final decision layer (Jain & Wallace 2019).

What to Look for in This Head:

  • Negation Binding: Notice how the token "not" attends heavily to "bad", allowing the model to capture the phrase boundary.
  • [CLS] Representation: The classification token at position 0 accumulates sentiment polarity across all tokens.
  • Multi-Head Diversity: Different heads specialize in positional proximity, syntactic agreement, or discourse connectors like "but".

Stage 1

Bag of Words & TF-IDF (1970s - 1990s)

Discrete, sparse frequency counters that treat sentences as unordered bags of words.

Mathematical Principle:

$$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \log\left(\frac{|D|}{|\{d \in D : t \in d\}|}\right)$$

Core Strengths:

  • Extremely fast training and microsecond inference.
  • Interpretable feature coefficients (each word has an explicit linear weight).
  • No GPU or deep learning framework required.

Fatal Limitations:

  • Curse of Dimensionality: Vocabulary size dictates vector length (often 10,000+ sparse zeros).
  • Zero Semantic Generalization: "Great" and "wonderful" are as mathematically distant as "great" and "refrigerator".
  • No Word Order: "Dog bites man" and "Man bites dog" produce identical representations.
Stage 2

Dense Static Word Embeddings (2013 - 2017)

Distributed representations mapping words into continuous dense vector spaces (Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText).

Mathematical Principle:

$$J = \sum_{i,j=1}^V f(X_{ij}) \left( w_i^T \tilde{w}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij} \right)^2$$

Core Strengths:

  • Compact fixed dimension (typically 50 to 300 dimensions).
  • Captures semantic proximity and geometric analogies ($v_{\text{king}} - v_{\text{man}} + v_{\text{woman}} \approx v_{\text{queen}}$).
  • Substantially improves generalization across synonymous terms.

Fatal Limitations:

  • Average Pooling Dilution: Averaging sentence vectors washes out word order and negation.
  • Static Polysemy Problem: A word has only one vector, regardless of whether it's "bank of a river" or "financial bank".
  • Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV): Cannot represent unseen words without fallback to subword character n-grams.
Stage 3

Contextualized Transformers (2017 - Present)

Multi-Head Self-Attention architectures that compute bidirectional, contextualized representations for every token.

Mathematical Principle:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

Core Strengths:

  • Contextual Embeddings: Words change vector representation based on surrounding tokens.
  • Bidirectional Context: Captures dependencies regardless of distance in the sentence.
  • Handles Compositional Semantics: Correctly maps negation operators ("not bad") and discourse connectives ("but").

Trade-offs & Considerations:

  • Quadratic computational complexity $O(n^2)$ with sequence length.
  • Requires substantial memory, parameters, and modern compute hardware.
  • Attention maps show routing but do not furnish complete causal explanations.
